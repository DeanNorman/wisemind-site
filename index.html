Grounding tools

Distress lives in the body first: the heart rate, the breath, the nervous system. You can't talk yourself out of an adrenaline flood, so every tool starts with the body.

Breathe out longer.

Paced breathing makes each out-breath longer than the in-breath. That engages the vagus nerve, slows the heart and eases fight-or-flight within minutes.

  • Inhale 4s
  • Hold 4s
  • Exhale 6s

Questions

Why does WiseMind start with the body, not words?

In acute distress, fight-or-flight pulls resources away from the parts of the brain that handle reasoning, so talking yourself down rarely works in the moment. Slow exhales and cold water brake the heart through the vagus nerve. Once the body feels safe, skills and words can take hold.

Is WiseMind a replacement for therapy or medical care?

No. WiseMind is an in-the-moment companion for regulation and recovery. It is designed to sit alongside therapy, psychiatry and twelve-step fellowship, not replace them. In an emergency, call 112 or the Suicide Crisis Helpline.

How are the Cape Town meetings checked?

We review local AA and NA intergroup schedules every week, confirming start times, venue access and whether a meeting is open or closed with group secretaries.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. Every timer, skill and saved meeting is stored on your phone, so the app works in the mountains, during loadshedding or in airplane mode.

What data does the app collect?

None. There is no username, password, email or phone number, and no analytics trackers. Check-ins and preferences stay on your phone.
